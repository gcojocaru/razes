# Historical reference guide: northern Moldavia, 1495

First focused pass, 22 October 2026. The setting is a **fictional village in northern Moldavia in 1495**. This date is a production reference, not a claim that the household or its encounters are documented. The MVP still covers one year, with no political event simulation.

## Why this period

Keep the brief’s medieval setting and locate the household within a world of inherited, sometimes shared, landholding. The National Heritage Institute’s CIMEC article on Ștefan cel Mare describes a charter issued in **1495** confirming a sale between two families of răzeși [S2]. That is a useful contemporary object behind the modern summary: family groups, land transactions, and a distinction between an agreement and princely confirmation.

The article links a catalogue record naming Patiul and his relatives, Ion Buzda and his relatives, and Dodești. We did **not** inspect the charter transcription or the linked catalogue record: the catalogue subdomain was blocked. The game does not borrow these people or this village. Nor does this southern Moldavian transaction prove the details of a northern household. The northern location is a fictional design choice, informed by the available Suceava museum collections [S3].

The word **răzeș** needs care. DEX 2009 describes a free Moldavian peasant holding inherited land in common, and more broadly a free landholder. MDA2 lists a 1642 attestation [S1]. That date is a dictionary’s cited attestation, not proof that the social group began in 1642; conversely, a modern museum calling people in a 1495 document “răzeși” does not prove the document used that Romanian word. Use the term as the game’s modern historical description, without pretending to reproduce fifteenth-century speech.

## Terminology decisions

| Term               | Meaning and use                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Răzeș / răzeși** | Singular/plural. Free landholder in Moldavia, often sharing inherited rights with kin. Retain the title; explain it briefly in How to play [S1].                                                                   |
| **Ocină**          | Hereditary landholding in the dictionary. Useful in the research glossary; plain “inherited land” is clearer in English dialogue. A dictionary definition alone does not establish a family’s precise tenure [S7]. |
| **Uric**           | Has meanings involving hereditary estate/privilege and a document. Do not rename every paper “uric” or grant Dumitru authority to issue one [S7].                                                                  |
| **Megieș**         | Has historical free-landholder and neighbour senses. Keep “neighbour” in the English interface instead of adding an unexplained title [S7].                                                                        |
| **Clacă**          | Dictionaries include both obligatory labour and voluntary collective help. Do not use it as an automatic translation of the ox promise; the context and period would need verification [S7].                       |
| **Groș / groși**   | A historically relevant coin term, but this pass has no denomination-specific price evidence. Do not relabel the abstract Money units as groși [S8].                                                               |

Ion, Ana, Petru, Vasile and Dumitru remain fictional modern-readable names. The former “Cojocaru homestead” scene label is now “Your family’s homestead”; no dated evidence was established for assigning that fixed surname to this fictional household.

## Changes applied in this pass

- The footer, metadata and help locate the fiction in northern Moldavia in 1495.
- Petru’s event acknowledges his inherited share and distinguishes personal provisions from land rights.
- Dumitru’s event describes a local agreement between families and neighbours, without a purchased deed or official judgment.
- The ox payment memory explicitly means paying **to use** the ox, not buying the animal.
- Paid work is described as a “spell of work,” avoiding the suggestion that one season’s assignment equals one day.
- How to play identifies resource and labour abstractions. Event IDs, save schema, work capacity and the one-year scope are unchanged. Earlier saved journal prose remains the record of the original choice.

## Art handoff: what must be verified next

Keep the present assets as playable illustrations. Before replacing them, obtain a dated reference with location, object type, social context and catalogue/page identifier for each of these groups:

1. Rural dwelling construction and roof covering; stove/chimney arrangement; size and glazing of openings. A princely hall or a later vernacular house is not sufficient evidence.
2. Everyday men’s and women’s garments, footwear and head coverings. Separate peasant workwear from elite burial dress and later folk costume.
3. Plough/draught arrangement, sickle, repair tools, grain storage, cart and well construction.
4. Cereals and sowing/harvesting practice appropriate to the chosen locality and date.

For each asset, retain the source link, date range, the feature actually supported, and what the artist invented. Check image reproduction rights separately before copying source imagery. This pass adds links and paraphrased notes; it does not redistribute museum images or claim that generated art is historical evidence.

## Access and research limits

The library homepage at `biblioteca-digitala.ro` returned a Cloudflare challenge during this pass; no challenged content was accessed. The linked `clasate.cimec.ro` catalogue record returned a proxy denial; no alternate route was attempted. Source access to CIMEC’s main site, dexonline, and the museum succeeded. The environment configuration tool continued to report policy state as unknown, so successful individual fetches establish access only to those fetched pages; they do not prove that the saved network draft was published.

Still needed: a specialist review of rural tenure and women’s inheritance/property rights, exact local building and clothing evidence, and agricultural material culture. Do not infer those details from the few sources above. No taxes, military service, land sales or new legal mechanics are introduced into this MVP.

These modern dry-grain references are proxies, not measurements of medieval
Moldavian cultivars. For calorie accounting, game grams represent dry edible
grain; dehulling/milling are abstracted without a separate mass or energy loss.
Porridge water adds no calories. Cooking time remains gameplay tuning; the
person's calorie burn follows the body model below. Nutrients, spoilage and
digestibility are not simulated.

## Body and needs model — 25 October 2026

The farmer's needs follow from a simulated body in `godot/human.gd`:

| Quantity | Value | Source or reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body | 1.718 m, 65 kg, 15% fat, BMI 22 | Game choice: a lean working adult, as tall as the 3D farmer |
| Created bodies | 1.55–1.85 m, whole kilograms from BMI 18.5 to 30, 8–30% fat | Game choice for the creation screen: full health at the start (WHO's 18.5) up to obese, and a fat share from lean to heavy. Not checked against Moldavian skeletal or written records |
| Basal metabolism | 370 + 21.6 × lean kg kcal a day (1,563 at the start) | Katch–McArdle equation: needs no age or sex, and falls as starvation consumes lean mass |
| Activity cost (× basal) | sleep 1.0, idle 1.4, cooking 2.1, walking 3.2, sowing and harvesting 4.1 | [FAO/WHO/UNU 2004, *Human energy requirements*, Table 5.1](https://www.fao.org/4/y5686e/y5686e07.htm): idle uses light leisure, sowing and harvesting non-mechanized agricultural work |
| Hoeing (Plow) | 5.0 × basal | About 5 METs for moderate hoeing and digging in the [Compendium of Physical Activities](https://pacompendium.com/occupation/) |
| Moving | basal + (ratio − 1) kcal an hour per kg moved, body plus load | One MET is about 1 kcal per kg an hour, so walking and field work cost more for a heavier body or a full sack; for the starting body this equals the ratio × basal |
| Fat and lean loss | 9,400 and 1,800 kcal per kg; FORBES / fat kg of lean per kg of fat, FORBES = 10.4 kg | Hall, K. D. (2008), "What is the required energy deficit per unit weight loss?", *International Journal of Obesity* 32: 573–576, using Forbes' rule |
| Fat and lean gain | a third of a surplus lost in storing; two thirds of the gain fat | Calibrated so 84 days of 1,000 kcal surplus add about 8 kg, as in [Bouchard et al. 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2336074/) (mean 8.1 kg); the fat share is a game choice |
| Glycogen | 9 g per kg of lean mass at 4 kcal a gram (1,989 kcal) | A typical adult store of about half a kilogram in liver and muscle |
| Stomach | 1.5 L, porridge at 4 ml per g of dry grain, so 375 g | Game interpretation of a hearty meal's volume |
| Gut absorption | at most 2.5 × basal (163 kcal an hour at the start) | The alimentary limit of [Thurber et al. 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aaw0341): sustained expenditure above about 2.5 × BMR must draw on body stores |
| Health | full at BMI 18.5 or more, zero at 12.5 | 18.5 is the WHO underweight threshold; 12.5 is chosen so total starvation with water lasts about two months, as in the [1981 hunger strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Irish_hunger_strike), where Bobby Sands died after 66 days and three others after 60–61 |
| Sleep and fatigue | 8 hours clear a day; 16 idle hours build 70% and nine hours' hoeing 30% more; 80% pace from 70%, 60% from 90% | Game tuning around the usual 7–9 hour adult need; [Van Dongen et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12683469/) found cumulative deficits on 6 hours a night for two weeks |

Checked in the running model: an idle farmer with no food keeps full health
until day 26 and dies on day 60 at 36.9 kg (1.45 kg fat, 35.4 kg lean), where
the previous model killed them 50 hours after 2,000 kcal of "food energy" ran
out. A day of nine hours' hoeing, an hour each of walking and cooking, five idle
hours and eight hours' sleep burns about 4,250 kcal, 2.7 times basal: above the
2.0–2.4 FAO gives as sustainable for non-mechanized labour and above the gut's
3,900 kcal a day, so even eating at every chance the farmer loses weight until
lighter days balance it. A resting farmer fed at every chance for a day gains
about 0.15 kg, two thirds of it fat.

The creation screen's skin, hair and cloth colours (undyed and bleached linen,
grey and brown wool, madder red, woad blue) are invented palettes, not evidence
of fifteenth-century peasant dress or dyes.

Not modelled: water, hulls (about a quarter of oat weight) and milling losses,
threshing and dehulling labour, fitness and strength, sex and age, circadian
timing of sleep, heat and cold, illness and injury.
