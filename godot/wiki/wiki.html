Ștefan wiki

Ștefan is a farming and strategy game set on a 16 by 16 field of plots. Each plot is 10 by 10 metres (100 m²); the whole field covers 2.56 hectares. These pages explain the seasons, the weather and how to grow grain. Open them in the game with F1 or the Wiki button.

Each new field has procedural hills and ponds from the land chosen before the game: Steppe (hills up to 7 m, one pond), Hills (18 m, two ponds), Lakeland (11 m, four ponds) or Custom (0–24 m, 0–4 ponds). Dry, gentle hills can be farmed; water, wet banks and steep plots show why work is unavailable. The farmer walks around ponds for jobs and trips to camp. People and crops follow the ground, while the camp has a small level clearing. The ledger shows usable plots and water, so the farmable area varies between fields.

The year

A year has four seasons of three months. Each month has four weeks of seven days, so a year is 336 days. The game starts at 06:00 on the first day of spring. At normal speed a day lasts ten real minutes. Days follow the sun at Chișinău's latitude: spring and autumn begin at the equinoxes, when the sun rises at 06:00, midsummer days last almost 16 hours and midwinter ones about 8. Dawn and dusk colour the sky, a full moon lights the night, the camp fire glows in the dark, and the weather icon shows the moon on clear nights. The top panel shows the hour. Space pauses the calendar and the farmer together; 1, 2 and 3 select normal, double and quadruple speed. F3 changes the day length in debug builds.

Every day has its own weather: clear, cloudy, rain, storm or snow. Temperatures follow the modern climate of Chișinău, from about −1.5°C in midwinter to 22.5°C in midsummer, with warm, cold, wet and dry spells that last several days. Snow only falls at 1°C or colder.

Working the land

  • Plow grass, stubble or dead crops to make a field. The ground is frozen in winter.
  • Sow a plowed field with a crop during that crop's sowing months. Sowing reserves the crop's own grain from the inventory as seed.
  • Harvest a ripe crop. Its grain goes into the farmer's bag, then to the inventory when carried home. The field is left as stubble, which must be plowed again before sowing.

Right-click land to open its action wheel: Plow for grass, stubble or dead crops; a separate icon for each available crop on plowed land; Harvest for ripe crops. Only actions possible now appear, with plot counts and seed costs or harvest yields in kilograms. A growing crop or unavailable work shows its reason in the centre. Right-clicking a selected plot keeps the group, so mixed land offers actions for each eligible part. Click a segment, or use arrows/Tab and Enter to queue the work. Escape or an outside click closes the wheel. Choosing a crop queues sowing and remembers it for Sow and F. Time keeps running and the wheel refreshes as days change.

The farm panel shows illustrated icons for each action the selected plots allow. Hover an icon for its name, plot count and kilograms of grain it costs or brings. The wheel icon opens Actions; the closed ledger, grain sack and open book open Ledger, Inventory and Wiki. Grain totals are in Inventory. Plowed land stays plowed. Stubble and dead crops left alone turn back to grass after about 40 warm days; cold days count less and winter days not at all.

The farmer

Before the game starts, Play opens peasant creation: give your peasant a name and choose their height, weight, body fat and looks (skin, hair, beard and clothes), then Begin. The summary shows what the body means: lean mass raises the metabolism and holds more ready energy, fat lasts longer without food, and every kilogram makes walking and work dearer. Restart keeps the same peasant.

One person carries out the farm orders. The Farmer panel, headed with their name, shows their activity, work left, queued plots, energy, stomach, weight, BMI, fatigue, health and carried grain. Press H or Focus to find them, B or Body for the Body window, G to focus camp and open its menu, R to configure Sleep or wake early, and Backspace or Clear jobs to cancel. Sleep preserves unfinished work; cancellation returns unused seed and carried grain. Ordering the same plot again cannot duplicate its job.

A farm action takes time: three game hours to plow 100 m² by hand, half an hour to sow it and two hours to harvest it. Sowing reserves seed immediately, so meals and other orders cannot spend it again. The farmer collects the seed at camp, walks to the plot and finishes the work before the crop changes. Harvested grain travels in a sack before entering the inventory. Work is checked again on completion: a changed plot, closed sowing season or frost-killed crop can make an old order unusable.

Eating and resting are manual. Hunger, exhaustion and night do not interrupt work automatically. Click the camp to open its action wheel, using the same rings and controls as the field wheel, then choose Eat to open Prepare food, a window with the inventory's grain bags. Select a grain and enter a quantity in whole grams; the porridge preview shows calories and preparation time. Prepare & Eat Porridge reserves exactly that amount of the selected grain, then the farmer returns to camp to cook and eat it. The cooked porridge fills the stomach, so a portion must fit the room left: at most 375 g of grain on an empty stomach. A 300 g portion takes half an hour; time scales with quantity, and calories depend on the grain. The preview and bag tooltips show calories per 100 g of dry edible grain. Only unreserved grain is available, and a pending meal cannot be ordered twice. Opening or closing spends nothing; Escape closes the menu or window. There is no initial flour supply.

The camp wheel offers Eat and Sleep. Use arrows or Tab and Enter; Eat is focused first. Escape or a click on the hub or outside dismisses it without changing field selection.

Click camp and choose Sleep to choose how many hours to sleep, up to 24 with quarter-hour controls. The live preview shows restedness (100 minus fatigue), energy, weight, health and elapsed time including the walk home. Eight hours of sleep clear a full day's fatigue, while the body keeps using its basal metabolism: food calories come from eating. The preview includes travel costs and stops at death if you cannot survive the chosen duration.

Confirm Sleep to return to camp and sleep for the chosen hours, during daytime or night. The timer starts on arrival. Full recovery does not shorten it; unfinished farm work resumes when the time ends. Wake up in the Farmer panel or R ends sleep early. Opening or closing the window starts nothing; Escape closes it. Finish a pending meal before sleeping, and wake before preparing food. Inventory shows available grain and the Farmer panel shows reserved and carried seed. Porridge is the only recipe so far, prepared and eaten as one command.

The body

The farmer's needs follow from their body, by default 1.72 m tall, 65 kg with 15% body fat, a BMI of 22. Lean mass sets the basal metabolism, about 1,563 kcal a day, and every activity burns a multiple of it. Being awake builds fatigue, and harder work builds it faster:

Moving adds about 1 kcal an hour per kilogram moved for each step above rest, so a heavier body, or grain in the sack, costs more. The stomach holds about 1.5 litres, 375 g of grain cooked as porridge. The gut absorbs at most about 2.5 times the basal metabolism, about 160 kcal an hour, so a full stomach takes about nine hours, and working harder than that slowly uses up the body. Digested food refills energy, the body's glycogen: about 2,000 kcal when full. More than that is stored, a third of it lost in storing and two thirds of the gain as fat, so overeating adds weight slowly. When energy runs out, the body burns fat and muscle instead and weight falls. Health stays full down to a BMI of 18.5 and is gone at 12.5. With no food at all an idle farmer lives about two months, like the hunger strikers of 1981. Eating again rebuilds weight and health. Press B or Body in the Farmer panel for the Body window: build and change since the start, energy, stomach, fatigue and what each activity costs this body, updated as time passes.

Sixteen idle hours awake build 70% fatigue and a day with nine hours of hoeing 100%; eight hours of sleep clear it. From 70% fatigue the farmer works and walks at 80% pace, and from 90% at 60%. At zero health the game ends: a centred Game Over message appears, the calendar and farming stop, and time controls and debug skips cannot resume play. Restart or Enter begins a fresh farm.

The person is about 1.7 metres tall and the crop heights use the same scale as the 10-metre plots. Grass outside the field supports the camp and is not selectable farmland. The camp and clothes are original artistic interpretations.

This is the first human simulation. Energy use and starvation follow published physiology; work rates, fatigue and pace are game tuning. Cooking water, fuel, hulls and milling losses, diet quality and weather exposure are abstracted. There is one person, no village growth, no vegetables and no saved gameplay progress yet. Debug time skips advance the person as well as the crops.

How crops grow

A crop grows each day the temperature is above its base, fastest at its best temperature. Rain helps and sunny days dry it out: oats gain the most from rain, and millet barely minds a dry spell. A crop dies on any day at or below its frost limit, whether it is still growing or already ripe, so harvest before the frost. Wheat and rye are winter crops: sown in autumn, they grow a little, wait out the winter and ripen the next summer.

Typically barley ripens in early summer, oats in mid to late summer, millet in late summer or early autumn, rye the next early summer and wheat after it. About one winter in five is cold enough to kill wheat. You start with last year's grain: 50 kg of barley, 40 of oats and rye, 37.5 of wheat and 9 of millet. That is seed for 25 plots of each and 30 of millet, but it is also your only food until the first barley ripens, about day 110: sow too much and you go hungry.

Harvest size

A plot is 100 m², one hundredth of a hectare. Quantities are shown in kilograms, accurate to one gram, so sowing millet costs 0.3 kg per plot. The table gives a plot's harvest on fully fertile soil in an average season, where every cloudy day counts as average. Each day a crop grows, rain helps fill its grain and sun dries it, as much as the crop cares for water, so every harvest comes out different:

  • Most years give 70–110% of the full harvest, about nine tenths on average, and a wet year up to a third more. Oats suffer most in a dry year, down to half; millet barely changes.
  • On average that is three to five times the seed back, as in medieval manor accounts and 18th-century Russian black-earth records, and millet, sown thin, more than ten times.
  • Worn soil gives less on top of that (see Soil).
  • Select a growing plot and hover its status to see the kilograms it is on course for; a ripe plot's status tooltip gives its exact harvest. The ledger's Fields tab adds them up by crop.

Soil

Every harvest wears the soil: barley and millet take 30% of its fertility, oats and rye 25% and wheat 35%. Worn soil gives a smaller harvest, though oats and rye mind it least. Land without a crop recovers 25% a year. Grow barley or millet on the same plot every year and its harvest halves within about nine years; wheat wears the soil out faster, oats and rye more slowly. Let a plot rest one year in three and a spring crop's harvest holds.

Ledger and inventory

  • The ledger (L) has three tabs. Fields shows what is growing by crop, with growth, soil and the harvest it is on course for. Years shows each year's sown, frost-killed and harvested plots and the kilograms gathered and gained after seed; hover a year for each crop. Crops lists each crop's needs.
  • The inventory (I) shows the store as bags of grain, one slot each. A bag holds about 70 litres, so heavier grain fills it to more kilograms: wheat 55, rye and millet 50, barley 45, oats 32. Each crop fills its bags in turn. Every bag is an open burlap sack with a heap of its grain at the mouth, the crop's initial stamped on it and its kilograms underneath; the last bag, part-filled, sits lower and wider, with its weight in gold. Below the bags is the total. Press Stats for each crop's kilograms and bags, the plots it can sow and what this year has sown and harvested.

A first year

  1. In early spring, plow a few plots and sow barley or oats.
  2. Late in spring, sow millet on another plowed field.
  3. Harvest through the summer as each crop ripens.
  4. In autumn, plow the stubble and sow wheat or rye for next year.
  5. Rest some land each year to keep the soil fertile.

These crops and numbers are game choices, not verified history for fifteenth-century Moldavia. The weather follows today's climate, not a reconstruction of medieval weather.

Sound

Use Sound at the top right, or F10, in the menu or field. Mute all sound or adjust Master, Weather and Music. Use arrow keys on a focused volume slider; Escape closes the panel. Preferences are remembered. Wind, rain and quiet winter ambience blend as weather changes; thunder follows lightning. Pausing or speeding the calendar keeps sound at its natural speed.
